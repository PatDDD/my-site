AI is changing how I move from a question to an analysis, a dashboard, or a recurring workflow. Here is what that looks like in my daily work.

<div class="comparison-scroll" role="region" aria-label="My data workflow before and now" tabindex="0">
<table class="workflow-comparison">
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Work</th><th scope="col">Before</th><th scope="col">Now</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><th scope="row">Analysis</th><td>Write SQL and PySpark notebooks from scratch.</td><td>Review agent-generated analysis in notebooks, then develop reusable scripts and organize the work in repositories.</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">Dashboards</th><td>Build in Tableau.</td><td>Build Streamlit apps and HTML pages, with automated, scheduled refreshes.</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">Workflows</th><td>Write Airflow workflows with PySpark or dbt.</td><td>Keep the same stack; use AI to turn existing analysis into recurring workflows.</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">Code review</th><td>Peer review.</td><td>Peer review plus AI review with Claude Code.</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>

The biggest shift is where I spend my effort: less on the first draft, more on checking the result and making it useful beyond a single request. Notebooks remain part of the process, with more emphasis on reusable scripts and repositories that can support dashboards and scheduled workflows.

AI does more of the drafting. Review still belongs in the process—including peer review.
